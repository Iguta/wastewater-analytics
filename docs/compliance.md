# Compliance map

Regulators and procurement offices gate wastewater software on evidence trails, read-only access and documented AI behavior, and a simple deterministic model can show all three where a black box cannot. Wording: "designed to support" only. No assessment has been done (see below).

| Framework | What it requires (source opened) | Dragonfly feature that supports it | Gap before a real deployment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EPA CMOM and SSO consent decrees | EPA's guide sets criteria for assessing collection-system capacity, management, operation and maintenance; some states mandate it ([Wisconsin DNR](https://dnr.wisconsin.gov/topic/wastewater/CMOM.html): 8 elements incl. maintenance activities and annual self-audit). Consent-decree terms are case-specific, and the EPA guide PDF would not parse: UNVERIFIED. | Every alert carries provenance (A§7, A§10.4 item 8) and a draft work order (A§10.4 item 9): a timestamped maintenance record. | Records live in the UI only; no export to a CMMS or audit log. IDEM requirements not reviewed. |
| NPDES permit SSO reporting | [40 CFR 122.41(l)(6)-(7)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/40/122.41): noncompliance that may endanger health or the environment is reported orally within 24 h and in writing within 5 days; other noncompliance goes with monitoring reports. | `HIGH_LEVEL` safety strip is never inhibited (A§4.8, gate G4). Raw timestamps and signal ids support an after-the-fact timeline (A§10.4 item 5). | Dragonfly does not detect overflows or file reports (A§1 not-building list). Permit terms vary. |
| IEC 62443 (3-3, 4-1, 4-2) | [ISA series page](https://www.isa.org/standards-and-publications/isa-standards/isa-iec-62443-series-of-standards): 3-3 system security requirements and levels; 4-1 secure product development lifecycle; 4-2 technical requirements for IACS components. Requirement text is paywalled: UNVERIFIED. | Read-only by design, no write path to control systems (A§1, A§8.1). CODEOWNERS-protected gates and `make verify` on every PR (A§6.3, A§11.1) are development-process evidence. | No threat model, authentication or role model (A§10.1: no accounts), no vulnerability-disclosure or patch process. No 4-1 or 4-2 assessment. |
| AWIA 2018 s2013 | [EPA](https://epa.gov/waterresilience/americas-water-infrastructure-act-risk-assessments-and-emergency-response-plans): community water systems serving more than 3,300 people assess risk and resilience (incl. monitoring, automated systems, operation and maintenance) and review every 5 years. Wastewater is not named on that page. | Read-only monitoring and evidence-quality flags (A§4.9) could feed an O&M resilience section. | Scope is drinking-water systems; relevance to wastewater is by analogy, or for utilities running both. |
| StateRAMP (now GovRAMP) / FedRAMP | [GovRAMP](https://govramp.org/): tiered verification against NIST controls (40 to 300+) for cloud providers. [FedRAMP](https://www.fedramp.gov/): marketplace of authorized services; the authorization process itself is UNVERIFIED. | Detector and API run locally with no cloud dependency; the LLM is optional and swappable for a local one (judge-qa, A§8.4). | Not a cloud service today (A§1: no cloud deployment). A hosted version would need a third-party assessment. |
| NIST AI RMF 1.0 | [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework): voluntary framework; functions Govern, Map, Measure, Manage (named in the overview page's figure text; the PDF itself was not opened). | `docs/MODEL_CARD.md` (intended use, not-for, training data); agent grounding check on every answer (A§8.4, A§10.4 item 10); 19/20 agent-eval gate (A§11.2); forbidden claims (A§6.7). | No post-deployment drift or incident monitoring; no named AI risk owner at the utility. |
| EU AI Act | [Annex III](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/): AI used as safety components in the management and operation of critical infrastructure, incl. supply of water, is high-risk. Obligations (Arts. 8-27) not opened: UNVERIFIED. | Advisory only, never a safety component: HIGH_LEVEL is untouched (A§4.8). Deterministic, versioned, reproducible (gate G3). | A UK or EU deployment needs a legal read on whether advisory use is in scope. |
| ISA-18.2 / IEC 62682 | [Yokogawa summary](https://blog.yokogawa.com/blog/implementing-alarm-management-per-iec-62682-standard) (not the standard): 10-stage lifecycle from philosophy to audit; an alarm calls for operator action. | One attention queue with fixed status states (A§10.2, A§4.11); safety strip never inhibited; alerts are advisory and separate from SCADA alarms. | No alarm philosophy document or KPI reporting on alert rates beyond the benchmark (A§6.2). |
| NIST SP 800-82 Rev. 3 | [NIST](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/82/r3/final): Sept 2023 guide to securing OT (layouts, threats, countermeasures). | Sits above SCADA and reads only on/off timestamps and level (A§1); Ignition view is read-only (A§10.8). | Network placement (DMZ, one-way transfer) untested; needs a site security review. |
| State Revolving Funds | [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/cwsrf/clean-water-state-revolving-fund-cwsrf-planning-assessment): CWSRF can finance wastewater utilities' asset-management planning as well as capital spending. Indiana rules UNVERIFIED (PDF not parsed). | Per-pump capacity trend and draft work orders support an asset-management program (A§4, A§10.4). | Eligibility of software subscriptions varies by state program; not confirmed. |

## What we do NOT claim

- No certification, attestation or regulatory approval under any framework above.
- No assessment by a regulator, third-party assessor or IEC 62443 assessor has been done.
- The demo runs on UK public data (Wessex) plus replayed Indiana scenarios, not on a US utility's systems.
- Dragonfly does not detect overflows, file permit reports or replace SCADA alarms.

## Pitch line

"Utilities under an SSO consent decree must prove proactive maintenance. Every Dragonfly alert is that proof: timestamped, sourced, versioned, with a draft work order a human approves."
